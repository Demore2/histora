# What If You Were Born as Baibars? (YouTube long-form #05)

## Teaser
What if you were Baibars?
At fourteen, you'd be sold at a slave market, and sent back, because of one bad eye.
You'd beat a king of France in the streets of a city.
You'd stop the Mongols, the army that had crushed half the world.
And then you'd kill the sultan you fought beside, and take his throne.
So how does a slave boy nobody wanted end up ruling Egypt and Syria?

## I · The steppe
You're born around 1223, far north of the Black Sea, on the grass steppe where the Kipchak Turks live.
Your people live in tents, on horseback, with their sheep and their horses.
You can ride before you can really walk.
Boys learn to shoot a bow from the saddle, and to sleep under the open sky.
Then, when you're a teenager, the Mongols come.
They sweep across the steppe, and your family runs.
Thousands of Kipchaks are caught on the way.
You're caught, put in chains, and sold.

## II · Eight hundred coins
You're taken south, to the slave markets of Anatolia and Syria.
You're tall, with dark skin, a loud voice, and bright blue eyes.
But one of those eyes has a white spot in it.
In the city of Hama, a buyer takes you, looks closer, and gives you back.
Damaged goods.
Then an emir named Aydakin buys you, for eight hundred silver coins.
He's a crossbowman, a bunduqdar, and his name sticks to you for the rest of your life: Baibars al-Bunduqdari.
Soon you're sold on again, to the sultan of Egypt himself.
He keeps his best slave soldiers in a fortress on an island in the Nile, and you become one of them.
They're called the Mamluks, which literally means "owned".
You learn to ride, to shoot an arrow backwards from a galloping horse, and to obey.
You're a slave, but you're a slave with a sword.

## III · A king in the streets
In 1249, a king of France lands in Egypt with a crusader army.
His name is Louis the Ninth, and later the Church will make him a saint.
He has brought thousands of knights, and ships full of horses.
He takes the port of Damietta, and marches up the Nile.
His brother's knights don't wait for the rest of the army.
In February 1250, they charge into the town of Mansoura.
And you're waiting for them.
You open the gates and let them ride in.
Then the narrow streets close around them like a trap.
From the rooftops come arrows, stones and pots.
The king's own brother dies in those streets.
A few weeks later, Louis the Ninth is captured, a king in chains.
And you're still in your twenties.

## IV · The blood oath
The new sultan doesn't trust the Mamluks who just saved his country.
He gets drunk, and starts cutting the heads off candles with his sword, shouting the names of the men he'll kill next.
So the Mamluks strike first.
You're one of the men who attack him.
The last sultan of Saladin's family is dead, and slave soldiers now rule Egypt.
But they turn on each other.
Your commander, Aqtay, is murdered in the palace.
And you have to run for your life.
For six years you live in exile, offering your sword to whichever ruler in Syria will pay for it.

## V · The Mongols
Meanwhile, the Mongols keep coming.
In 1258, they take Baghdad, the greatest city of the Islamic world.
They kill the caliph, and, according to the chroniclers, hundreds of thousands of people.
Then they march into Syria.
Aleppo falls.
Damascus opens its gates without a fight.
And a Mongol envoy rides into Cairo with a letter for the new sultan, Qutuz.
Surrender, it says, or we will do to you what we did to Baghdad.
Qutuz has the envoys cut in half, and hangs their heads on the gate of Cairo.
Now there's no way back.
And Qutuz needs every fighter he can get, even you.
So you come home, and the two of you make peace.

## VI · The spring of Goliath
On the third of September 1260, the two armies meet at a spring in Palestine called Ain Jalut, the spring of Goliath.
You lead the front of the army.
Your job is to find the Mongols, hit them, and then pretend to run.
It's an old steppe trick, one the Mongols use themselves.
The Mongols chase you, straight into the hills, where the rest of the Mamluk army is waiting.
The Mongols almost break through anyway.
Qutuz throws his helmet to the ground and shouts, "O Islam!", and his men charge.
The Mongol general Kitbuqa is killed.
For the first time, a Mongol army has been beaten in a great battle, and the whole world hears about it.

## VII · The hunt
After the battle, you ask Qutuz for one reward: the city of Aleppo.
He says no.
A few weeks later, on the way back to Cairo, Qutuz stops to hunt.
You ride up to him, take his hand as if to kiss it, and hold it tight.
Then the other emirs strike.
The man who stopped the Mongols is dead, and his killers choose a new sultan.
They choose you.
From now on you call yourself al-Malik al-Zahir, the victorious king.
Cairo was decorated for Qutuz.
It cheers for you instead.

## VIII · The sultan
You rule for seventeen years, and you barely ever sit still.
You bring a survivor of the Abbasid family to Cairo, and make him caliph, so your throne has a holy blessing.
You build a postal service with relay horses and carrier pigeons, so a letter from Damascus reaches you in about four days.
You sometimes travel in disguise, to see your own lands with your own eyes.
Your emblem is a lion, and you put it on your coins, your bridges and your buildings.
And you go after the crusader castles, one by one.
Caesarea, Arsuf, Safed, Antioch.
The great castle of Krak des Chevaliers had never fallen.
So you send its knights a letter, signed by their own commander, telling them to surrender.
The letter is forged.
They surrender anyway.

## IX · The cup
In 1277, you beat the Mongols one more time, deep in Anatolia.
Then, in Damascus, you drink a cup of kumis, fermented mare's milk, the drink of the steppe you came from.
A few days later, you're dead.
You're about fifty-four years old.
One story says the cup was poisoned, and meant for someone else.
You're buried in Damascus, under a dome that's now part of a library.
And for centuries afterwards, storytellers in the coffee houses of Cairo and Damascus tell your life as a legend, night after night.
The boy who was sent back for one bad eye.
