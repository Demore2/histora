## Voice-over (clean, for TTS)
In 73 BC, about seventy gladiators broke out of their school — armed with kitchen knives. Their leader: a Thracian named Spartacus. Rome trapped them on a mountain. They braided ropes from wild vines, climbed down the cliffs, and attacked from behind. Tens of thousands of slaves joined him. He beat army after army. He marched north toward the Alps — then turned back. No one knows why. In 71 BC, Crassus crushed them. His body was never found. That mountain? Vesuvius. That's thirty seconds of history.

## Fact notes
- Breakout from Lentulus Batiatus's school at Capua, 73 BC; 78 men "with choppers and spits from a cook-shop" (Plutarch, *Crassus* 8); "about seventy" (Appian, *Civil Wars* 1.116).
- Thracian origin — Plutarch, *Crassus* 8. **Not used:** "born a slave" and the line "I am not a slave" — not attested (the app's story text has both; flag for a fix).
- Besieged on Vesuvius by Glaber; descended on ladders of wild vines and took the camp from behind — Plutarch, *Crassus* 9; Frontinus 1.5.21.
- Followers: ancient estimates 70,000–120,000 (Appian 1.116–117) — hence the safe "tens of thousands".
- Marched north, defeated Cassius near Mutina, then turned south instead of crossing the Alps; his reason is unknown — Plutarch 9; Appian 1.117.
- Final defeat by Crassus in Lucania/Apulia, 71 BC; body never found — Appian 1.120. (6,000 crucified on the Appian Way left out for length, not taste.)
- Vesuvius buried Pompeii about 150 years later — sets up `05-pompeii.md` as a follow-on.
