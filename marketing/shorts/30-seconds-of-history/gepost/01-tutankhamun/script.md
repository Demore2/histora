## Voice-over (clean, for TTS)
The most famous pharaoh was almost wiped from history. Crowned at about eight. Dead at nineteen. His burial was so rushed, the paint was still wet when they sealed the tomb. The kings after him struck his name from the official lists. Later, workmen built huts right over his tomb. And that's what saved him. Robbers emptied almost every royal tomb in the valley. His survived nearly intact — until 1922, when Howard Carter held up a candle and saw "wonderful things." That's thirty seconds of history.

## Fact notes
- Became king around 1332 BC aged about eight or nine; died around 1323 BC aged about 18–19 — standard chronology (Reeves, *The Complete Tutankhamun*).
- Wet paint: microbial spots on the burial-chamber walls show the plaster/paint was still damp when the tomb was sealed — Getty Conservation Institute study (2019).
- Left out of the king lists (Abydos, Karnak) under Horemheb, along with Akhenaten and Ay — standard.
- Workmen's huts over the entrance: built during work on the tomb of Ramesses VI (KV9), c. two centuries later.
- Tomb was entered by robbers at least twice soon after the burial and resealed — hence "nearly intact", not "untouched".
- "Wonderful things": Carter's own later account (26 Nov 1922); his diary wording differs slightly — the app attributes it "as he later told the story".
