## Voice-over (clean, for TTS)
You say Julius Caesar's name every summer. In 49 BC he led his army across the Rubicon, into Italy. That was illegal — and it meant civil war. He won, and Rome made him dictator for life. He fixed the calendar — one year lasted four hundred and forty-five days. Then, on the Ides of March, more than sixty conspirators closed in. He was stabbed twenty-three times. That year, Rome renamed the month of his birth. Today we call it July. That's thirty seconds of history.

## Fact notes
- Rubicon crossing, January 49 BC; a governor bringing his army out of his province into Italy was treason — Suetonius, *Julius* 31–33.
- Named *dictator perpetuo* in early 44 BC — Suetonius 76; coinage of 44 BC.
- 46 BC was lengthened to 445 days to realign the calendar ("the last year of confusion") — Censorinus 20; Macrobius *Sat.* 1.14.
- More than sixty conspirators; 23 wounds, only one judged fatal by the physician Antistius — Suetonius 80, 82.
- Quintilis renamed Iulius in 44 BC by a law of Mark Antony, after his death; Caesar was born in that month — Macrobius *Sat.* 1.12.34; Suetonius 76.
- Kept off-screen on purpose: "Et tu, Brute?" (Shakespeare, not Roman sources).
