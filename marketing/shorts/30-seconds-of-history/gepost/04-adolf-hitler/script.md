## Voice-over (clean, for TTS)
Hitler never won a majority. He was handed power. In 1923, he tried to seize power by force. He failed, got five years for treason, and was out in nine months. Then the Depression hit. Six million Germans lost their jobs. Even at their peak, the Nazis won thirty-seven percent of the vote. In January 1933, the old elite made him chancellor anyway. They thought they could control him. Within two months, he could make laws without parliament. The war he started cost tens of millions of lives. His regime murdered six million Jews.

## Fact notes
- Never a majority: best free Reichstag result 37.3% (July 1932); 33.1% in November 1932; even the
  March 1933 election, held under terror, gave 43.9%. Kershaw, *Hitler 1889–1936: Hubris*.
- Beer Hall Putsch, Munich, 8–9 November 1923; collapsed the next day. Sentenced 1 April 1924 to five
  years' fortress confinement for high treason; released 20 December 1924 (about nine months after the
  verdict). Wrote most of *Mein Kampf* (vol. 1) in Landsberg — left out of the voice for time.
- Unemployment: about six million registered unemployed in early 1932 (peak of the Depression).
- 30 January 1933: appointed chancellor by President Hindenburg, pushed by Papen and the conservative
  right, who expected to "frame" and control him — Turner, *Hitler's Thirty Days to Power*.
- Enabling Act, 23 March 1933 (52 days later): the cabinet could pass laws without the Reichstag.
- War: WWII began with the invasion of Poland, 1 September 1939; total deaths commonly put at 70–85
  million ("tens of millions"). Holocaust: about six million Jews murdered (USHMM).
- The fire scene is an illustration of the night-time violence of these years, not a specific event;
  no date label is put on it except "1933" for the section.
