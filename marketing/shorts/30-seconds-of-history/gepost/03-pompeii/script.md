## Voice-over (clean, for TTS)
The people of Pompeii didn't know they lived next to a volcano. Vesuvius was covered in vineyards. Nobody remembered it erupting. In 79 AD, a cloud rose over the mountain — "like a pine tree," wrote a teenager across the bay. Stones rained down for hours. Most people fled. Next morning, a surge of burning gas and ash swept through the city — faster than anyone could run. Pompeii's famous figures aren't bodies. They're plaster, poured into the hollows people left in the ash. That's thirty seconds of history.

## Fact notes
- Vesuvius was not recognised as an active volcano; Strabo (5.4.8) only noted burnt-looking rock; slopes cultivated with vines (Martial 4.44 describes them after the eruption) — no eruption in living memory.
- Date given only as "79 AD": the traditional 24 August (manuscripts of Pliny) is disputed; a charcoal inscription found in 2018 points to October. Do not name the day.
- Pine-tree cloud: Pliny the Younger, *Letters* 6.16 — he was 17, at Misenum across the bay.
- Pumice fell for hours on day one; most inhabitants appear to have left — the roughly 1,150 victims found so far are a fraction of a population of ~11,000 (estimates vary).
- Pyroclastic surges reached Pompeii early on the second morning — modern volcanology (Sigurdsson et al.); "faster than anyone could run" is safe (surges travel tens to hundreds of km/h).
- Plaster casts: Giuseppe Fiorelli's method, from 1863 — the casts contain skeletal remains inside but are plaster, not preserved bodies.
