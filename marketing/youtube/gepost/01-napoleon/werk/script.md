## Hook
He was mocked for his accent at school.
Twenty-five years later, he crowned himself emperor.
And he died on a rock in the middle of the ocean, nearly two thousand kilometres from the nearest coast.
This is the life of Napoleon Bonaparte, in five minutes.

## The boy from Corsica
He was born in 1769 on Corsica, an island France had taken over from Genoa only the year before.
His first language was Corsican, not French.
At nine he was sent to a military school in France.
The other boys laughed at his accent and at his poverty.
So he read.
History, mathematics, and the lives of Caesar and Alexander.
At sixteen he left school as an artillery officer.

## The Revolution
Then France exploded.
In 1789 the Revolution swept away the old order, and suddenly talent mattered more than birth.
In 1793 the port of Toulon was held by the British.
A young captain named Bonaparte placed the guns that drove their fleet out of the harbour.
He was made a general.
He was twenty-four.
Two years later, royalist crowds marched on the government in Paris.
He met them with cannon fire.
Historians would later call it a whiff of grapeshot.

## Italy and Egypt
In 1796 he married Joséphine, a widow with two children.
Two days later he left to command the army in Italy.
It was ragged and unpaid.
Within a year it had beaten Austria again and again.
Then he sailed for Egypt, with soldiers and more than a hundred scholars.
At the Pyramids, as he later told it, he said: forty centuries look down on you.
His men found the Rosetta Stone.
But the British admiral Nelson sank his fleet, and Napoleon slipped back to France without his army.

## First Consul
In November 1799 he and his allies overthrew the government.
Soldiers cleared the parliament hall.
At thirty he was First Consul, and in practice the ruler of France.
In 1800 he crossed the Alps to surprise the Austrians.
The famous painting shows him on a rearing white horse.
In reality he rode a mule, led by a local guide.
In 1804 came the Civil Code: one set of laws for all of France.
Versions of it are still in force in many countries today.

## Emperor
That same year, in Notre-Dame, the pope came to crown him.
Napoleon took the crown and placed it on his own head.
A year later, at Austerlitz, he beat the armies of two emperors in a single day.
But at sea, Nelson destroyed the French and Spanish fleets at Trafalgar.
Napoleon ruled the land.
Britain ruled the waves.
He made his brothers kings across Europe.
And because Joséphine gave him no heir, he divorced her and married an Austrian archduchess.

## Russia
In 1812 he invaded Russia with about six hundred thousand men.
The Russians retreated and burned what they left behind.
In September he reached Moscow.
It was almost empty.
Then it burned.
He waited more than a month for a peace offer that never came.
Then he turned back, into the Russian winter.
Cold, hunger and Cossack raids destroyed his Grand Army.
Estimates vary, but perhaps one man in six came home.

## Elba and Waterloo
His enemies closed in.
In 1814 they took Paris and sent him into exile on the island of Elba.
Ten months later, he escaped.
Near Grenoble, a regiment was sent to stop him.
He walked up to them alone, and as the story is told, he said: if any of you wants to kill his emperor, here I am.
They joined him.
For a hundred days he ruled again.
Then, on the eighteenth of June 1815, at Waterloo, the British and the Prussians beat him for the last time.

## Saint Helena
This time the British took no chances.
They sent him to Saint Helena, a speck of rock in the South Atlantic.
He spent six years there, dictating his memories and quarrelling with his jailer.
He died in 1821, aged fifty-one.
Nineteen years later his body was brought back to Paris, where he lies under the dome of Les Invalides.
And he was not short.
About one metre sixty-nine, average for his time.
The tiny, furious Napoleon was invented by British cartoonists.
His empire lasted barely ten years.
His laws are still with us.
